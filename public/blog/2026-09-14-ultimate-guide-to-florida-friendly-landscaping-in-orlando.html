The Ultimate Guide to Florida-Friendly Landscaping in Orlando

Discover how Florida-Friendly Landscaping™ principles can transform your Orlando property into a beautiful, sustainable oasis that conserves water, reduces maintenance, and protects our environment.

If you're an Orlando homeowner or property manager looking to create a stunning landscape that thrives in Central Florida's unique climate while conserving resources, Florida-Friendly Landscaping™ (FFL) is your answer. This comprehensive guide will walk you through the nine principles of FFL and show you how to implement them on your property.

What is Florida-Friendly Landscaping™?

Florida-Friendly Landscaping™ is a sustainable approach to landscape design and maintenance developed by the University of Florida's IFAS Extension program. It's specifically designed for Florida's climate, soil conditions, and environmental challenges. The program focuses on creating beautiful landscapes that:

  • Conserve water and protect water quality
  • Minimize fertilizer and pesticide use
  • Reduce maintenance requirements and costs
  • Support local wildlife and pollinators
  • Prevent soil erosion and runoff

The 9 Principles of Florida-Friendly Landscaping™

1. Right Plant, Right Place

The foundation of successful Florida-Friendly Landscaping is selecting plants that are naturally adapted to Central Florida's conditions. This means considering:

  • Light requirements: Full sun, partial shade, or full shade
  • Water needs: Drought-tolerant vs. moisture-loving species
  • Soil type: Sandy, clay, or loamy conditions
  • Mature size: Ensuring plants have room to grow without constant pruning
  • Cold hardiness: USDA Zone 9a/9b appropriate varieties

Top Native and Florida-Friendly Plants for Orlando:

Trees:

  • Live Oak (Quercus virginiana): Majestic shade tree, extremely drought-tolerant once established
  • Southern Magnolia (Magnolia grandiflora): Evergreen beauty with fragrant white blooms
  • Dahoon Holly (Ilex cassine): Native evergreen, attracts birds, tolerates wet areas
  • Crape Myrtle (Lagerstroemia indica): Summer color, drought-resistant, low maintenance

Shrubs:

  • Coontie (Zamia integrifolia): Native cycad, extremely drought-tolerant, host plant for Atala butterfly
  • Firebush (Hamelia patens): Year-round blooms attract hummingbirds and butterflies
  • Simpson's Stopper (Myrcianthes fragrans): Native evergreen, fragrant flowers, berries for birds
  • Muhly Grass (Muhlenbergia capillaris): Stunning pink fall color, low water needs

Groundcovers:

  • Beach Sunflower (Helianthus debilis): Bright yellow blooms, salt-tolerant, spreads quickly
  • Sunshine Mimosa (Mimosa strigillosa): Native groundcover, nitrogen-fixing, powder-puff flowers
  • Asiatic Jasmine (Trachelospermum asiaticum): Dense coverage, shade-tolerant, low maintenance

2. Water Efficiently

Orlando receives approximately 50 inches of rainfall annually, but distribution is uneven. Efficient irrigation is critical for plant health and water conservation:

Smart Irrigation Strategies:

  • Install a smart irrigation controller: Weather-based systems adjust watering schedules based on rainfall, temperature, and humidity
  • Use drip irrigation for beds: Delivers water directly to root zones, reducing evaporation by up to 50%
  • Zone by water needs: Group plants with similar requirements (hydrozones)
  • Follow St. Johns River Water Management District restrictions: Currently twice-per-week watering in most areas
  • Water early morning: 4-10 AM reduces evaporation and disease pressure

Our professional irrigation services include system design, installation, and maintenance to ensure optimal water efficiency for your Orlando property.

3. Fertilize Appropriately

Florida's sandy soils are naturally low in nutrients, but over-fertilization causes pollution and harms waterways. Follow these best practices:

  • Test your soil: The Orange County Extension Office offers affordable soil testing
  • Use slow-release fertilizers: Provides steady nutrition over 2-3 months
  • Apply the right amount: More is not better—follow label instructions
  • Observe Orlando's fertilizer blackout: No nitrogen or phosphorus fertilizer June 1 through September 30
  • Keep fertilizer off hardscapes: Sweep up any granules that land on driveways or sidewalks
  • Leave grass clippings: Natural nitrogen source that reduces fertilizer needs by 25%

4. Mulch Properly

Mulch is one of the most beneficial practices in Florida-Friendly Landscaping. A 2-3 inch layer of organic mulch provides multiple benefits:

  • Reduces water evaporation by up to 70%
  • Suppresses weeds and reduces herbicide needs
  • Moderates soil temperature
  • Prevents soil erosion and compaction
  • Adds organic matter as it decomposes
  • Creates a professional, polished appearance

Best Mulch Types for Orlando:

  • Melaleuca (Eucalyptus) mulch: Long-lasting, natural pest resistance, locally produced
  • Pine bark nuggets: Attractive appearance, good longevity, doesn't float as easily
  • Pine straw: Traditional Southern choice, good for acid-loving plants
  • Cypress mulch: Very durable but environmentally controversial—choose reclaimed sources

Important: Keep mulch 2-3 inches away from tree trunks and plant stems to prevent rot and disease. Never create "mulch volcanoes" around trees.

5. Attract Wildlife

Central Florida is home to incredible biodiversity. Your landscape can support pollinators, birds, butterflies, and beneficial insects:

Creating Wildlife Habitat:

  • Plant native species: Co-evolved with local wildlife for optimal benefit
  • Provide food sources: Nectar plants for butterflies and hummingbirds, seed-bearing plants for birds, host plants for butterfly larvae
  • Add water features: Birdbaths, small ponds, or recirculating fountains
  • Create shelter: Dense shrubs, brush piles (in appropriate locations), and nesting boxes
  • Avoid pesticides: Especially broad-spectrum insecticides that harm beneficial insects
  • Leave some "wild" areas: Unmowed sections with native grasses and wildflowers

Star Performers for Central Florida Wildlife:

  • Milkweed species: Essential host plant for Monarch butterflies
  • Firebush: Hummingbird magnet, year-round nectar source
  • Beautyberry (Callicarpa americana): Purple berries attract 40+ bird species
  • Passionvine (Passiflora incarnata): Host plant for Gulf Fritillary butterflies

6. Manage Yard Pests Responsibly

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) reduces reliance on chemical pesticides through prevention, monitoring, and targeted treatment:

IPM Strategies for Orlando Landscapes:

  • Select resistant varieties: Many newer cultivars resist common diseases
  • Maintain plant health: Healthy plants naturally resist pests and diseases
  • Monitor regularly: Early detection prevents small problems from becoming large ones
  • Use physical barriers: Row covers, copper tape for slugs, tree bands for weevils
  • Encourage beneficial insects: Ladybugs, lacewings, and parasitic wasps control pests naturally
  • Spot-treat when necessary: Target specific pests rather than broadcast spraying
  • Choose low-toxicity options: Horticultural oils, insecticidal soaps, and biological controls

7. Recycle Yard Waste

Orange County diverts millions of pounds of yard waste from landfills annually. You can participate by:

  • Composting: Turn leaves, grass clippings, and small branches into nutrient-rich soil amendment
  • Grasscycling: Leave clippings on the lawn—they decompose in days and provide free fertilizer
  • Using curbside collection: Orange County offers free yard waste pickup
  • Creating mulch: Run leaves and small branches through a chipper/shredder
  • Building wildlife habitat: Stack branches to create shelter for beneficial creatures

8. Reduce Stormwater Runoff

Orlando's frequent heavy rains can cause erosion and carry pollutants into our lakes and rivers. Florida-Friendly Landscaping minimizes runoff through:

Stormwater Management Techniques:

  • Rain gardens: Depressed planting areas that capture and filter runoff
  • Permeable paving: Allows water to soak into the ground rather than running off
  • Swales and berms: Direct and slow water flow across the landscape
  • Rain barrels: Capture roof runoff for irrigation use
  • Proper grading: Ensures water flows away from buildings but doesn't concentrate in problem areas
  • Extensive mulching: Increases infiltration and reduces erosion

Our drainage solutions help Orlando property owners manage stormwater effectively while creating attractive landscape features.

9. Protect the Waterfront

If your Orlando property borders a lake, pond, or wetland, special considerations apply:

  • Maintain a littoral zone: Keep a 10-foot buffer of native plants along the water's edge
  • Avoid fertilizing near water: Extra-wide setbacks prevent nutrient runoff
  • Use native aquatic plants: Stabilize shorelines and filter runoff naturally
  • Never dump yard waste in water: Decaying vegetation depletes oxygen and harms fish
  • Install erosion control: Coir logs, riprap, or specialized plantings prevent bank collapse

Designing Your Florida-Friendly Landscape

Ready to transform your Orlando property? Here's how to get started:

Step 1: Assess Your Site

  • Map existing features (buildings, trees, utilities)
  • Note sun exposure throughout the day
  • Identify wet and dry spots
  • Evaluate soil type and drainage
  • Consider views and privacy needs

Step 2: Define Your Goals

  • Low maintenance vs. high-impact design
  • Wildlife habitat priority
  • Outdoor living spaces
  • Curb appeal enhancement
  • Water conservation targets

Step 3: Create Functional Zones

  • High-use areas: Lawn for recreation, outdoor dining spaces
  • Low-use areas: Native plantings, naturalized areas
  • Service areas: Utility access, trash storage, compost bins

Step 4: Select Your Plants

Choose species that match your site conditions and maintenance preferences. The University of Florida's Florida-Friendly Plant Database is an excellent resource.

Step 5: Design Your Irrigation System

Plan efficient irrigation that delivers the right amount of water to each zone. Consider drip systems for shrub beds and high-efficiency rotary nozzles for turf areas.

Maintaining Your Florida-Friendly Landscape

Once established, Florida-Friendly Landscapes require less maintenance than traditional designs, but some care is still necessary:

Monthly Tasks:

  • Monitor irrigation system performance
  • Check for pest and disease issues
  • Weed early and often (easier when small)
  • Prune as needed to maintain shape and health

Seasonal Tasks:

  • Spring: Apply slow-release fertilizer, refresh mulch, plant warm-season annuals
  • Summer: Monitor water needs, watch for pests, deadhead flowers
  • Fall: Apply fall fertilizer, plant cool-season annuals, divide perennials
  • Winter: Prune deciduous plants, plan next season's changes, protect tender plants during freezes

Common Florida-Friendly Landscaping Mistakes to Avoid

1. Over-Watering

More plants die from too much water than too little. Let soil dry between waterings and avoid daily shallow watering that encourages shallow roots.

2. Wrong Plant Selection

A sun-loving plant in shade or a moisture-loving species in dry soil will struggle no matter how much you care for it. Always match plants to site conditions.

3. Improper Mulching

Too much mulch smothers plants, while too little provides inadequate benefits. Maintain 2-3 inches throughout beds and keep mulch away from stems and trunks.

4. Fertilizing During Summer

Orlando's summer fertilizer blackout (June 1-September 30) exists because heavy rains wash excess nutrients into waterways. Follow the rules—your plants will be fine.

5. Planting Too Close Together

Consider mature size when spacing plants. Crowded plantings require excessive pruning and compete for resources.
